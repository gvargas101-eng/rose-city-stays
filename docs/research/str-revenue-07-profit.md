# STR profit improvements beyond nightly rate — Rose City Stays

**Portfolio:** 11 short-term and corporate-rental properties, Tyler, Texas  
**Research date:** 30 September 2026  
**Decision rule:** Treat an add-on as successful only when its *incremental contribution* is positive and it does not harm conversion, stay quality, reviews, or operational reliability. Gross fee revenue is not profit.

Rose City Stays should use a small, clearly explained set of fees and availability-controlled upsells rather than attempt to monetize every guest touchpoint. Airbnb allows hosts to configure cleaning, pet, and extra-guest fees; its permitted calculation methods differ by fee. It also says a cleaning fee is for general post-stay cleanup, and hosts should not impose a fee or penalty for guests failing specified checkout chores. [1] Airbnb supports a separate short-stay cleaning fee for one- and two-night stays. [2]

## Profit lens for every change

**Evidence.** HSMAI’s revenue-management practitioners explicitly advise moving beyond revenue-only measures toward profit measures such as gross operating profit (GOP/GOPPAR), and say ancillary offers should be operationally deliverable and connected to live inventory. Their early-arrival/departure pilots were capacity controlled rather than offered without limit. [3]

**Recommendation.** Build a property- and channel-level add-on P&L. For each booking, calculate:

> **Incremental contribution = fee/add-on revenue − OTA or payment fees − incremental labor − supplies/laundry − incremental utilities − damage/claim/refund cost − expected displacement or service-recovery cost.**

Do not call a fee “margin” until it is measured after these costs. Allocate shared labor consistently (for example, actual cleaner invoice plus any documented inspection minutes), and report both per booking and per occupied night. Review every proposed fee for a 30–60 day test against a comparable pre-test period and a control group of similar Rose City Stays homes where practical.

## Fee and upsell design

| Lever | Evidence and recommendation | Gross revenue vs. contribution-margin effect | Operational gate and consumer-trust risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pet-friendly stays and pet fee** | **Evidence:** Airbnb permits flat, per-pet, per-night, and per-pet-per-night fees, but says the fee is for *expected* pet cleaning and may not exceed the base nightly rate. [4] **Recommendation:** Accept pets only in homes that cleaners can reliably reset. Track declared pets, incremental cleaning minutes, linen rewash, odor/remediation supplies, damage incidents, and lost future nights. Set a simple disclosed fee from the measured *expected cleaning cost*, then retest quarterly rather than copy a market rule of thumb. | The fee raises gross booking revenue; it becomes contribution only to the extent it exceeds incremental expected cleaning and minor-wear cost without reducing conversion or length of stay. Do not use it as a substitute for damage recovery. | Show the fee, permitted pet count, pet rules, and what the fee covers before booking and again pre-arrival. Airbnb prohibits pet- or hair/dander-related fees for service animals; applicable law and Airbnb’s Accessibility Policy also govern. [5] Surprise charges, or treating a pet-cleaning fee as a damage penalty, are high-trust risks. |
| **Extra guest fee** | **Evidence:** Airbnb permits a flat extra-guest fee above a host-selected default number of guests. [1] **Recommendation:** Set the included occupancy at the true “comfortable” capacity, not fire-code maximum. Price the incremental fee from guest-specific consumables, linen/towel loads, utilities, wear, and any added setup/inspection time. Accurately disclose bed types and the maximum occupancy. | This is gross revenue from higher occupancy. Contribution is fee less the incremental variable cost and any increased claim, noise, or review-recovery cost. | Never sell a nominal capacity that produces cramped sleeping arrangements or unavailable linens. Clear capacity and sleeping configuration protect accuracy and value ratings; vague or punitive after-booking charges do not. |
| **Early check-in / late check-out** | **Evidence:** HSMAI identifies flexible arrival/departure as a potentially high-margin ancillary service, but its cited pilots controlled availability (typically one or two hotel rooms daily) and depended on live inventory plus operational training. [3] **Recommendation:** Offer a paid, *conditional* time window in pre-arrival messaging only after the departure/arrival pattern and cleaner schedule are known. Begin with a strict daily cap by property/cleaner route; waive the fee only when it is operationally free and commercially sensible. | Potential high contribution because the service may require little incremental cost. But an offer that causes a rushed or failed turnover can destroy contribution through overtime, refunds, damage to reviews, or a displaced same-day booking. | The promise must say “subject to operational confirmation,” specify the actual time, and be declined automatically where it conflicts with a same-day turnover, maintenance, or cleaning quality check. Do not sell an unconfirmed convenience. |
| **Cleaning and linen economics** | **Evidence:** Airbnb differentiates short-stay and standard cleaning fees, and says expected housekeeping belongs in the fee while certain unexpected cleaning is handled through AirCover. [2] Expedia Group advises that standard, well-explained cleaning, pet, and extra-guest fees are more acceptable than vague or excessive charges, and specifically warns against charging for basics such as bath linens. [6] **Recommendation:** Keep a standard cleaning fee that tracks the verified turnover cost by home size; consider Airbnb’s lower short-stay fee setting only when it improves total-price competitiveness and still protects contribution. Treat linen service as an internal cost-control program, not a guest-facing bath-linen fee. Standardize par levels, count losses/stains, record pounds/loads or vendor invoices, and use a checkout inspection checklist before cleaning begins. | A cleaning fee is revenue collected to recover a per-turn cost, not automatically profit. Margin improves when cost per *successful, inspection-passed turnover* falls without lowering cleanliness. Longer stays can reduce turnover frequency and may improve contribution even at a discounted nightly price. Expedia Group notes weekly/monthly discounts can reduce turnover hassle; validate that against the portfolio’s actual margin. [6] | Keep total-price competitiveness under review, especially for short stays. Avoid a separate linen, administrative, internet, utility, or multiple small nuisance fees. They erode perceived value and can trigger complaints even if the arithmetic is positive. |
| **Damage deposits / damage protection** | **Evidence:** On Vrbo, a guest may be asked for a card on file, an upfront refundable deposit, or property damage protection. A card-on-file claim must be filed within 14 days; a refundable deposit is paid at booking and refunded after 14 days if no claim is filed. [7] Vrbo also offers optional guest-paid PDP choices, with published price/coverage examples, and requires documented reasonable repair costs within 14 days for deposit claims. [8] Airbnb’s Host Damage Protection is up to $3 million for certain unexpected damage/cleaning but is not insurance; it excludes normal wear and ordinary checkout cleaning and needs evidence and action within 14 days. [9] **Recommendation:** Use the channel-native path for each channel; retain timestamped pre-stay/checkout photos, inventory and invoices, and a same-day inspection workflow. Use a deposit/hold only where risk history supports it, and evaluate whether avoided losses plus recovery rate exceed conversion loss and admin time. | A refundable deposit is **not revenue**. Its financial value is avoided unrecovered damage minus claims administration, refund friction, and conversion loss. Damage protection has no contribution benefit until claims recovery and premium/fee effects are measured. | A deposit creates cash-hold friction. On Vrbo, the amount is displayed before booking, but the guest may wait 14 days plus financial-institution release time for refund. [7] State the amount, purpose, inspection timing, evidence process, and any platform terms upfront. Claim only documented, reasonable damage—not ordinary wear or routine cleaning. |

## Transparent pricing and guest experience protect conversion and reviews

**Evidence.** Expedia Group’s vacation-rental guidance says travelers understand standard fees but may view basic utility or internet charges as superfluous; it recommends clear detail for necessary fees, no vague labels such as “administrative,” no excessive/non-proportionate charges, and generally no more than one or two separate fees. [6] Airbnb’s guest ratings cover overall stay, cleanliness, accuracy, check-in, communication, location, and value. It urges accurate listing information, low-effort checkout, availability for guests, and review responses. [10]

**Recommendation.** Set a portfolio fee architecture: (1) a cleaning fee calibrated by unit/turnover profile, plus (2) only the applicable pet or extra-guest fee. Put unavoidable recurring costs into the all-in nightly economics rather than add administrative, Wi-Fi, utility, or basic-linen lines. Confirm the same rules in listing copy, OTA fee settings, rental agreement (where used), booking confirmation, and pre-arrival message. Audit a sample search and checkout flow on every channel monthly so the displayed total matches the intended policy.

This is not merely a reputation initiative. Cleanliness, accuracy, check-in, communication, and value are measurable review dimensions. Make the operational promise modest and repeatable: current photos and amenity list; precise parking/check-in instructions; a staffed escalation path; no surprise checkout labor; and a documented response to every review theme. Do **not** claim that a review intervention will raise rankings or revenue unless Rose City Stays’ own test demonstrates it.

## Operational availability is a revenue-control system

**Evidence.** Airbnb directs hosts to block dates they cannot host, synchronize calendars, set preparation time, and keep availability current to avoid cancellations and scheduling conflicts. [11] Expedia Group reports that Vrbo partners using Instant Booking and calendar syncing saw 60% more bookings in its internal January–December 2022 data; it also says syncing can prevent double bookings and Instant Booking appears in more/higher search results. This is platform-specific observational/internal evidence, **not a forecast for Tyler or this portfolio**. [12]

**Recommendation.** Maintain one source of truth for availability across every channel, with twice-daily exception review during same-day-turnover periods. Each home needs: cleaner capacity and backup coverage, property-specific reset duration, inspection sign-off, maintenance blocks, check-in cutoff, and an owner-use lock process. Offer same-day bookings and early/late upsells only when those gates are met. When a gate fails, close inventory rather than create a preventable cancellation or poor arrival. The lost gross revenue from a closed night is visible; the avoided cost of refunds, re-accommodation, claims, and review damage must be captured in the availability scorecard.

## Measurement scorecard and test rules

Measure weekly by property, stay-length band (1–2, 3–6, 7–27, and 28+ nights), channel, and pet/non-pet where relevant. Aggregate to portfolio only after unit-level review.

| Metric | Calculation / use |
|---|---|
| **Ancillary gross revenue per occupied night** | (Cleaning + pet + extra-guest + early/late fee revenue) ÷ occupied nights. Report each fee separately so fee growth cannot hide a weak lever. |
| **Ancillary contribution per booking and per occupied night** | Fee revenue less channel/payment fees and all incremental costs. This is the decision metric; track a zero or negative result explicitly. |
| **Pet economics** | Pet bookings as % of bookings; declared pets per stay; pet-fee contribution; incremental post-pet clean minutes/cost; odor/damage incident and recovery rate. |
| **Turnover and linen economics** | Cleaner invoice/labor minutes, supplies, laundry cost, linen replacement, inspection pass rate, and re-clean rate per turnover. Track cost per occupied night *and* per turnover to avoid falsely crediting longer stays to housekeeping efficiency. |
| **Flexible-arrival economics** | Requests, offers, accepts, fulfillment rate, fee contribution, overtime, same-day conflict/reversal, and guest issue/review mentions. Never count an offered but unfulfilled upsell as revenue. |
| **Fee-friction funnel** | Listing view → booking conversion, booking lead time, cancellation, support contacts/refunds, and review “value”/fee mentions before vs. after each change. Use directionality and sample size; do not infer causation from one week. |
| **Reliability** | Calendar-sync conflicts, owner/host cancellations, double-bookings, maintenance blocks, on-time check-in rate, and time from guest contact to first response/resolution. |
| **Review quality** | Overall plus cleanliness, accuracy, check-in, communication, and value ratings; coded complaint themes; public-response completion. Tie changes to the affected property/operating process. [10] |
| **Damage recovery** | Incidents per 100 stays; documentation-complete rate; claims filed within channel deadline; approved/recovered amount; unrecovered loss; deposit/PDP conversion impact. |

A change should be rolled back or redesigned if incremental contribution is negative, if fulfillment/review-quality deterioration offsets the gain, or if the explanation cannot be made clear before booking. Test one fee architecture or operational rule at a time, document the date and affected listings, and preserve the guest-facing copy used in the test.

## Recommended order of implementation

1. **Baseline first (two to four weeks):** create the add-on P&L, turnover/linen log, damage evidence workflow, review-theme codebook, and channel fee audit. No new fee amount is justified until Rose City Stays can identify its incremental cost.
2. **Remove friction before adding monetization:** replace vague/multiple fees with the limited, disclosed architecture; correct capacity, bedding, checkout, and pet copy; make calendar and cleaning coverage reliable.
3. **Pilot the reversible levers:** test pet fee calibration and extra-guest threshold in operationally suitable homes; separately pilot conditional early/late requests with a daily cap. Compare contribution and fee-friction metrics by stay-length and channel.
4. **Standardize what survives:** deploy only profitable, well-fulfilled offers. Keep deposit/damage-policy choices channel-specific and evidence-led, then review quarterly for claims, conversion, and trust effects.

## Consumer-trust and compliance red flags

- **Do not add charges after booking** unless the guest has a clear, voluntary, documented change; a surprise fee is not an upsell.
- **Do not price pets as an undisclosed damage penalty** or charge service-animal-related pet/hair/dander fees on Airbnb. [4] [5]
- **Do not bill routine checkout cleaning or normal wear as damage.** These are outside Airbnb Host Damage Protection’s ordinary coverage concepts; use a routine cost model instead. [9]
- **Do not collect or describe a deposit as income.** Clearly identify hold, refund timing, claim deadline, and evidence standard. [7] [8]
- **Do not sell early/late access that cleaning and inspection cannot support.** Fulfillment reliability is part of the product.
- **Do not rely on platform-wide or hotel evidence as a Tyler forecast.** Validate every fee level, conversion effect, and operational assumption in Rose City Stays’ own data.
