# Rose City Stays — STR Revenue Management: KPI, Forecasting, Pace & Reporting Memo

**Scope.** This memo recommends a lightweight revenue-management operating system for an owner-operator of **11 Tyler, Texas homes** serving short-term and corporate-rental demand. It does **not** assert Tyler market benchmarks or prescribe a universal rate/occupancy target; those require current, comparable-market data. “Night” below means one sellable home-night.

> **Decision principle:** optimize **realized net revenue per available night** and operating fit—not calendar fill alone. Hospitality authorities treat occupancy, ADR, and RevPAR as complementary: a high occupancy result can be economically poor if achieved by rate cuts or excess variable cost ([CoStar/STR, “Occupancy Rate,” published June 12, 2024](https://www.costar.com/products/str-benchmark/resources/data-insights-blog/what-occupancy-rate)).

## 1. Measurement contract: define once, then do not drift

**Recommendation (Rose City Stays):** create one reservation-level export from the PMS/channel manager, refreshed daily, with: property; booking/arrival/departure/cancellation dates; booking status and cancellation date; channel; gross nightly accommodation charge; discounts; refunds; taxes; cleaning/pet/other pass-through fees; OTA/merchant/payment fees; payout; active minimum stay at booking; and owner/maintenance blocks. Snapshot the **on-the-books (OTB)** calendar daily at a fixed local time. Retain reservations after cancellation rather than deleting them.

Use a **stay-date** ledger for realized performance and an **as-of-date** ledger for forward pace. Reconcile a monthly statement to OTA payouts/bank deposits. Exclude taxes and pass-through cleaning fees from room/accommodation revenue unless the metric is explicitly labeled otherwise. Count a home-night as *available* only when it was deliberately sellable; log owner, maintenance, regulatory, and self-imposed blocks separately. Otherwise an apparent occupancy improvement can simply be an inventory withdrawal.

This adapts standard lodging definitions: CoStar/STR defines occupancy as occupied rooms divided by rooms available, ADR as room revenue divided by rooms sold, and RevPAR as room revenue divided by rooms available ([occupancy](https://www.costar.com/products/str-benchmark/resources/data-insights-blog/what-occupancy-rate), [ADR](https://www.costar.com/products/str-benchmark/resources/data-insights-blog/what-average-daily-rate-adr-and-how-calculate), [RevPAR](https://www.costar.com/products/str-benchmark/resources/data-insights-blog/what-revenue-available-room-revpar-and-how); all published June 12, 2024 except RevPAR, January 23, 2020). Apply “room” as “home-night.”

## 2. Core KPI scorecard

| KPI | Formula (Rose City definition) | Interpretation and action | Measurement risks / controls |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Occupancy** | `occupied stayed nights ÷ sellable available nights × 100` | Demand captured. Review with ADR and RevPAR; do not reward filling nights at any price. CoStar/STR uses occupied ÷ available ([source](https://www.costar.com/products/str-benchmark/resources/data-insights-blog/what-occupancy-rate)). | Owner blocks, maintenance blocks, cancellations, extensions and same-day turns distort it. Report sellable and total calendar capacity side by side. |
| **ADR** | `gross accommodation revenue for stayed paid nights ÷ occupied stayed nights` | Average price actually paid per occupied night before channel/payment costs. It shows rate realization among sold nights, not unsold inventory. CoStar/STR defines ADR as room revenue ÷ rooms sold and restricts it to revenue-generating rooms ([source](https://www.costar.com/products/str-benchmark/resources/data-insights-blog/what-average-daily-rate-adr-and-how-calculate)). | Do not include taxes, deposits or cleaning fee in a room-rate ADR. Segment by property, channel, weekday/weekend, LOS, lead-time bucket and corporate vs. transient. |
| **RevPAR / RevPAN** | `gross accommodation revenue for stayed nights ÷ sellable available nights` = `ADR × occupancy` | Primary gross top-line efficiency metric; in STR call it **RevPAN** if preferred, but retain the formula. It reconciles rate and fill. CoStar/STR calls RevPAR room revenue ÷ available rooms ([source](https://www.costar.com/products/str-benchmark/resources/data-insights-blog/what-revenue-available-room-revpar-and-how)). | A portfolio average can hide one underperforming home; show total plus property-level distribution. It is revenue, **not profit**. |
| **Realized ADR** | `realized accommodation revenue ÷ occupied stayed nights`, where realized revenue = booked accommodation charge **minus** post-booking discounts, refunds, disputed/returned payments, and cancellation-related reversals; exclude taxes/pass-throughs | Separates “what was quoted/booked” from what actually remained after stay completion. Track **booked ADR** separately (accommodation revenue OTB ÷ OTB booked nights) for forecast management. A widening booked-to-realized gap points to concessions, cancellations, modifications or reconciliation problems. | This is an internal management definition, not a universal OTA standard. Freeze the policy; report it by **stay month** after the cancellation/refund tail closes. |
| **Net revenue** | `realized accommodation revenue + retained ancillary revenue − OTA/host commissions − merchant/payment processing − refunds/chargebacks − channel-specific booking costs` | Cash/economic yield after acquisition and payment costs. Use **net RevPAN** = net revenue ÷ sellable available nights as the price/occupancy decision metric. IDeaS defines a net rate as price less commission/transaction cost ([source](https://ideas.com/tools-resources/hotel-glossary-terms/)). | Do not label it NOI or profit: cleaning, utilities, labor, repairs, furnishing depreciation and fixed costs are not included unless explicitly added. Keep a second “contribution after variable stay cost” measure if those costs are reliably captured. |
| **Average length of stay (ALOS)** | `occupied stayed nights ÷ completed reservations` | Indicates booking pattern, cleaning frequency and calendar fragmentation. IDeaS defines LOS as number of nights stayed / arrival-to-departure difference ([source](https://ideas.com/tools-resources/hotel-glossary-terms/)). Compare it with net RevPAN and turn costs: longer is not automatically better if it displaces higher-value dates. | One corporate stay can dominate an 11-home portfolio. Report median LOS and distribution (1, 2–3, 4–6, 7–27, 28+ nights), not only the mean. |
| **Lead time** | Per reservation: `check-in date − booking-confirmed date` (days); report median and mean by arrival month/channel/property | The time a guest booked ahead. Airbnb defines its dashboard’s booking lead time as the average time between booking and check-in ([source](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/2714)). Use it to decide when a blank future calendar is normal versus concerning. | Use the original confirmed booking date; flag date modifications. Means are skewed by corporate/monthly stays; medians and percentile bands are safer. |
| **Booking window** | Portfolio/segment distribution of lead time for a specified arrival period, e.g., `% of final booked nights acquired at 0–3, 4–7, 8–14, 15–30, 31–60, 61+ days` | Operationally, treat **lead time** as the reservation-level measure and **booking window** as the aggregate booking curve. IDeaS defines the window as the timeframe between booking and arrival and pace as booking accumulation over time ([source](https://ideas.com/tools-resources/hotel-glossary-terms/)). | The terms are often used interchangeably; label the definition in the dashboard. Booking behavior shifts with events, channel mix and policy, so do not apply an old curve indefinitely. |
| **Cancellation rate** | **Count:** `cancelled reservations ÷ confirmed reservations × 100`; **value:** `cancelled gross accommodation value ÷ confirmed gross accommodation value × 100` | Counts booking reliability; value shows revenue exposure. Booking.com defines its dashboard rate as cancellation **value** divided by total booking value for the selected period ([source](https://partner.booking.com/en-us/help/growing-your-business/analytics-reports/using-performance-analysis)). Review booking-date cohorts and cancellation timing (days before arrival). | Do not mix guest cancellations, host cancellations, modifications, no-shows and payment failures. Cancellation denominator must be stated: booking-date cohort is best for forecast calibration; stay-date gives realized-month context. |
| **Conversion** | Channel-specific: `completed bookings ÷ listing-page views × 100`; also track `listing-page views ÷ search impressions × 100` | Diagnoses funnel break: visibility, listing appeal, or booking friction/price. Airbnb describes the funnel as first-page search impressions → search-to-listing → listing-to-booking and calls overall conversion the share of unique search viewers who book ([source](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/2714)); Booking.com describes conversion as page views turning into bookings ([source](https://partner.booking.com/en-us/help/growing-your-business/analytics-reports/using-performance-analysis)). | Never combine differently defined OTA conversion denominators. Use the platform’s definition, same date basis, and enough views before treating movement as a signal. Availability and rank changes confound it. |
| **Minimum-stay compliance** | `reservations whose LOS meets/exceeds the active minimum-stay rule at confirmation ÷ reservations subject to that rule × 100` (also show `compliant nights ÷ subject nights`) | Audits whether channel/PMS rules were actually enforced and whether approved exceptions are deliberate. IDeaS defines Min LOS as a control requiring a reservation to meet/exceed a stated stay length ([source](https://ideas.com/tools-resources/hotel-glossary-terms/)); Airbnb permits minimum-night rules and custom rules by check-in day ([source](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/880)). | Store the rule version and exception reason (owner-approved, platform exception, alteration). Do not count a legitimate two-night booking against a one-night gap rule as noncompliance. Pair with orphan-gap nights and net RevPAN—perfect compliance can still leave revenue idle. |

### Two practical KPI views

1. **Daily forward view (arrival-date, as of today):** available nights; OTB nights/revenue/ADR; net OTB revenue; pickup and cancellations since snapshot; pace vs matched baseline; forecast occupancy, ADR, gross/net RevPAN; restrictions; exception flags.
2. **Monthly realized view (stay-date):** above metrics plus booked vs realized ADR, channel costs, cancellation cohorts, ALOS distribution, conversion, forecast error, and minimum-stay exceptions. Show **portfolio total, each home, and comparable cohorts** (e.g., bedroom count/amenity/corporate suitability)—never only a blended portfolio average.

## 3. Forecasting and pickup/pace monitoring

### Evidence and the recommended model

**Evidence.** HSMAI/IDeaS describes forecasting as a continuing prediction of future occupied rooms and revenue; its short-range approach incorporates OTB business plus pickup/wash and considers history, seasonality/day of week, events, lead time/pace, cancellations and LOS ([HSMAI Revenue Ready Forecast course](https://academy.hsmai.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/11/2017/09/course-6-forecasting-hsmai-presentation-1.pdf)). Boston University hospitality research likewise concludes that forecasts based on booking curves/pace are more resilient to changing booking windows than a fixed pickup factor, and warns to monitor accuracy when behavior shifts ([Webb, 2021](https://www.bu.edu/bhr/2021/06/29/traveler-booking-windows-and-revenue-management-forecasting/)).

**Recommendation — three horizons, not one magic forecast.**

| Horizon | Method and formula | Owner-operator use |
|---|---|---|
| **0–21 days: arrival-level rolling forecast** | For each property/arrival date: `forecast occupied nights = current net OTB nights + expected gross pickup − expected future cancellations/no-shows`, capped at sellable nights. Estimate pickup and cancellation probabilities from matched historical cohorts: property cohort, arrival weekday, season/event flag, channel, LOS and **days-to-arrival**. `forecast revenue = net OTB realized-value estimate + expected pickup × expected realized ADR − expected cancellations/refunds`. | Daily exceptions: price/rule changes, turn staffing, late cancellation risk. Use human event notes (sports, university, hospital/corporate demand, weather disruption) rather than silently letting anomalies enter the baseline. |
| **22–90 days: pace-curve forecast** | Start with OTB at each lead-time bucket, then apply a matched booking curve/expected remaining pickup. For a simple transparent version: `forecast = TY OTB ÷ historical fraction of final nights normally OTB at same DTA`, then adjust for known event/supply changes and cancellation likelihood. HSMAI illustrates this pace calculation concept ([source](https://academy.hsmai.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/11/2017/09/course-6-forecasting-hsmai-presentation-1.pdf)). | Rate ladders, minimum-stay settings, corporate outreach and event controls. Refresh twice weekly and record overrides/reasons. |
| **91–365 days: budget/scenario forecast** | Use matched historical season/day-of-week baseline plus explicit scenarios for available inventory, events, corporate demand, and supply—not daily price micromanagement. Produce base/downside/upside revenue and occupancy ranges. | Owner cash planning and base-rate/rule calibration. Reforecast monthly; do not portray far-future point estimates as precise. |

At this scale, begin with **transparent cohort medians or weighted averages**, not an opaque machine-learning model. Use property-level history only when enough comparable observations exist; otherwise pool an explicitly defined cohort and retain property fixed differences. Test forecast accuracy after each stay month: `error = actual − forecast`; **bias** = mean error (systematically high/low); **MAE** = mean absolute error; and revenue/RevPAN error. Review accuracy by horizon (0–7, 8–21, 22–60 days), property, weekday, event flag and channel. A forecast that looks good at portfolio total may be materially wrong at a single home.

### Pickup, pace, and decision triggers

**Definitions (evidence).** HSMAI defines **pickup** as bookings made since the last report and says a daily pickup/pace report covers bookings, room nights and revenue; it compares pace with the previous year ([source](https://academy.hsmai.org/glossary/pick-up-or-pace-report/)). For Rose City Stays, calculate both gross and net movement:

- `gross pickup nights = new confirmed OTB nights since prior snapshot`
- `net pickup nights = new nights + extension nights − cancelled nights − shortened nights` (and the equivalent revenue amounts)
- `pace variance (nights) = current OTB nights at DTA − matched-baseline OTB nights at same DTA`
- `pace index = current OTB nights at DTA ÷ matched-baseline OTB nights at same DTA × 100`

The baseline should match **arrival weekday, season, DTA, property/cohort, and known event status**. “Same date last year” alone is weak when weekday alignment, inventory, events, policies or channel mix changed. The 11-home portfolio is small: inspect raw reservation movements, not only a percentage index.

**Action ladder (recommendation; validate against actual performance):**

1. **Ahead of pace** and forecast occupancy/net RevPAN above the matched range: hold discounts, test a modest rate increase on unbooked nights, protect high-value stay patterns with a targeted Min LOS, and check that conversion/cancellations do not deteriorate.
2. **Behind pace** but still outside the usual booking window: do not automatically discount. Verify availability, channel sync, search visibility, listing changes, reviews and event assumptions; wait for the next matched pace checkpoint.
3. **Behind pace inside the normal booking window:** make one reversible change (rate fence, last-minute discount, Min LOS relaxation, gap rule, or channel exposure), log it, then compare incremental **net** pickup and conversion to a matched control/baseline.
4. **Orphan gap:** first relax/check-in or Min LOS only for the gap; discount only if its expected incremental net value exceeds the rate dilution/turn cost. STR specialist Beyond explicitly highlights minimum-stay flexibility for orphan nights and the tradeoff between longer stays and operating costs ([Beyond, “Revenue Management for Vacation Rentals,” August 5, 2026](https://beyondpricing.com/blog/revenue-management-for-vacation-rentals-the-guide)).
5. **High cancellation cohort:** forecast with the cohort’s wash, avoid treating gross OTB as final revenue, and review policy/channel/guest type before changing price.

## 4. Experiment design appropriate to 11 homes

**Do not infer causality from an isolated rate change.** Demand, events, booking curve, availability, placement, review changes, and channel mix all move together. Price has an additional ethical/channel-parity constraint: avoid arbitrary, simultaneous guest-facing price discrepancies for the same comparable offer.

**Recommended protocol**

1. **Pre-register one hypothesis and one decision.** Example: “For low-demand Sunday–Thursday arrivals 7–21 days out, reducing the Min LOS from 2 to 1 only when it creates no extra turnover will increase realized net RevPAN without lowering realized ADR or increasing cancellation rate beyond guardrails.” Define eligible homes/dates and a stop date before launch.
2. **Choose a comparable unit and control.** For listing-content or rule tests, use matched homes (bedrooms, quality, location/use case) or randomly assign eligible comparable home-date cells. For pricing, use matched arrival weekday/season/DTA cells and a holdout or alternating matched periods; do **not** compare a festival weekend to an ordinary one. With small n, prefer repeated, reversible tests over a claim from one week.
3. **Change one lever.** Keep base price, availability, cancellation policy, photos/title, promotions and Min LOS unchanged except for the experimental lever. Record PMS/channel sync time and exceptions.
4. **Measure the full funnel and profit proxy.** Primary: realized **net RevPAN** by stay date. Secondary: occupancy, realized ADR, conversion (channel-specific), net pickup by DTA, cancellation value rate, ALOS, turn count, and minimum-stay compliance. Pre-set guardrails (e.g., no host-cancellation, no occupancy-impacting service degradation, and a minimum acceptable net revenue/turn outcome).
5. **Wait for realization, then decide.** Compare completed/cancellation-matured stays to matched control/baseline; report effect in dollars and percentages, sample size, eligible dates, cancellation tail, and confounders. “Inconclusive” is a valid result. Roll out only when results are economically material and repeatable; otherwise revert or test a narrower condition.

**Suggested first test queue:** (a) gap-specific 1-night Min LOS versus a 2-night rule; (b) a transparent last-minute rate fence inside the observed booking window; (c) photo/order/title or amenity clarity to improve listing-page conversion; (d) length-of-stay discount only for low-demand periods. Do not test several at once. Airbnb’s platform supports custom Min LOS by check-in day ([source](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/880)), but capability is not evidence that a setting increases revenue—measure it.

## 5. Cadence and reporting package for an 11-home owner-operator

| Cadence / owner time | Required output | Decisions and exceptions |
|---|---|---|
| **Daily, 10–15 minutes** | Exception list for arrivals 0–21 days: new/cancelled/modified bookings; net pickup; OTB vs forecast; orphan gaps; properties below/above pace; channel sync or Min LOS failures. | Act only on defined exceptions; check same-day/next-day availability, price floor/ceiling, and turns. Snapshot before changes. No broad “daily repricing” without a reason log. |
| **Twice weekly, 45 minutes (e.g., Monday/Thursday)** | Arrival calendar 0–90 days, pace curves at 7/14/21/30/60/90 DTA, event notes, restrictions, channel comparison. | Approve measured rate/rule tests and event strategy; review all homes, but concentrate on exceptions. The second review catches late bookings/cancellations without creating daily noise. |
| **Weekly, 60 minutes** | Rolling 13-week forecast: OTB, expected pickup/wash, occupancy, booked/forecast ADR, forecast gross and net RevPAN; conversion and cancellation cohort trends; experiment log. | Reforecast; document overrides and owners; verify only intentional blocks reduce sellable inventory. |
| **Monthly close, 90 minutes within 5 business days** | Stay-month realized P&L-facing scorecard: gross/realized ADR, net revenue/net RevPAN, occupancy, ALOS distribution, channels/costs, cancellations, forecast bias/MAE, property/cohort comparison. | Reconcile PMS/OTA payout/bank data; retire/revise forecast cohorts; decide experiment rollout/reversal. Discuss exceptions, not only portfolio average. |
| **Quarterly, 2 hours** | 12-month pattern review, rate floors/ceilings/base-rate logic, segment/channel profitability, calendar/event assumptions, inventory/block audit and experiment learning. | Reset policy guardrails, corporate-rental strategy, benchmark/comparable cohort definitions and next quarter’s test backlog. |

### Minimum viable dashboard layout

- **Page 1 — Today:** 0–21 day exceptions, OTB, net pickup, cancellation alerts, orphan gaps, arrival/turn risk.
- **Page 2 — 90-day revenue calendar:** each home/date with sellable availability, OTB, forecast, pace index, gross/net RevPAN, ADR, Min LOS and experiment tag.
- **Page 3 — realized month:** property and portfolio KPI table; channel/segment waterfall from booked accommodation revenue to net revenue; booked vs realized ADR.
- **Page 4 — learning:** forecast error, pace curve movement, cancellation cohort, conversion funnel, experiment register and decision log.

## 6. Guardrails and limitations

- **No local benchmark was claimed.** Tyler targets should be set only after obtaining current, comparable STR data and validating listing similarity; local anecdotes and unverified market-rate assertions are not a benchmark.
- **Corporate rentals change denominators.** Report 28+ night stays separately and maintain a clear classification/policy for tax, utilities, cleaning and channel costs. A long stay can raise occupancy while reducing ADR or opportunity for higher-value nights.
- **Channel reports are not interchangeable.** Airbnb conversion is based on unique search visitors who book, whereas Booking.com frames conversion from property-page views; Booking.com’s cancellation rate is value-based ([Airbnb](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/2714); [Booking.com](https://partner.booking.com/en-us/help/growing-your-business/analytics-reports/using-performance-analysis)). Preserve source-specific denominators.
- **Revenue is not profit.** RevPAR/RevPAN excludes operating cost; net revenue still may exclude cleaning/labor/utilities. Use a documented contribution metric before choosing longer stays or discounts solely on top-line results.
- **Data volume is modest.** 11 homes produce noisy property-level results. Use matched cohorts, medians/ranges and repeat tests; do not call a single booking or one event weekend evidence.
